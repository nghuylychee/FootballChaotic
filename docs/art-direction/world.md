# World

**One city, one climb.** Every arena is a rung on the climb from a poor village on the outskirts to the top of the city. It is not lore for players to read; it is a set of facts that shows up in the pictures.

The game's text already tells this climb: GOAT PATH goes THE BLOCK → NIGHT CITY → THE LEGENDS, the Main Path goes from Village Green ("Where the dream begins") to World Stage, and Grandpa walked "this same road" fifty years ago.

## The city

- **An Asian coastal city with light Vietnamese touches.** Vietnamese details are small decorations (a conical hat, a plastic stool, a scooter, a shop sign, laterite), never a full Vietnamese setting. A player should sense an Asian country and wonder which one.
- **No name**, in the game or in these docs.

## What every arena shares

| Glue | In the picture |
|---|---|
| **The climb** | Places get bigger and richer as you rise: ground goes dirt → asphalt → tiles → a real pitch; lines go lime → chalk → paint → proper white lines; goals go tied sticks → scrap → welded frames → real goals; the crowd goes a few neighbours → a busy street → a full stadium; light goes sun → lamps and lanterns → floodlights |
| **Time of day follows the climb** | Daytime on the outskirts and in the block, night in the city, floodlights at the top |
| **The dream on the horizon** | The city skyline with the stadium in it, small from the outskirts, close downtown, all around you at the top |
| **One light** | The same direction everywhere ([palette and light](palette-and-light.md)) |
| **The neighbours** | The crowd is the people who live there, dressed for the place |
| **The street kit** | Things every street pitch has, in that rung's materials: plastic stools, a chalk scoreboard, a ball bag, a drink cooler, a parked scooter |
| **Local words** | Wall writing names real things of that place (a shop sign, a class number, a dock number), never generic words repeated across arenas |

GOAT PATH act 2 (Rooftop, Market, Plaza, Street, Cyber) must read as one city at night: one warm night light (lamps, lanterns, lit windows), not five unrelated palettes.

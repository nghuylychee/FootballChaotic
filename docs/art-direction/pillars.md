# Pillars

Four words for how the game should feel. A new arena, prop or colour that doesn't fit them doesn't go in.

- **Scrappy:** street football in real places, played with whatever is at hand. Chalk or lime lines, patched walls, makeshift goals, plastic stools.
- **Intense:** the game is a fight, and the place doesn't pretend otherwise. The stage is dim, dusty and heavy, never a sunny postcard. Nothing in the scenery should feel peaceful.
- **Readable:** the ball, the players and the goals always read first. The arena is the stage, not the show.
- **Punchy:** hits, shots, goals and Ultimates get the brightest colours, the strongest contrast and the shake. The arena never does.

Short form: **quiet stage, loud action.** The stage is quiet because it is dark and muted, not because it is calm.

## Rules out

- Bright, sunny, pastoral scenery; anything that reads as a cosy village postcard.
- Neon lighting, glowing floor grids and blinking screens in the arena.
- Anything in the arena brighter or more saturated than the players.
- Scenes so dark the play is hard to see.
- Sterile, polished places with nobody around.

## Where it comes from

- After a match, a player should say "that was intense".
- The intensity comes from the action, so the arena leaves room for the action to be the loudest thing on screen.
- The focus is gameplay: the ball, the players and the goals must read at a glance.
- Flash belongs to the action, never to the background.

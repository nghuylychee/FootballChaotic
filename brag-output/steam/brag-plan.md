# Street Football Chaos: Steam trailer #1 (Gameplay Trailer)

## Research: what the first Steam videos usually do
- **Steam auto-plays the first trailer, muted**, in a small box (~600px wide). The first 2 videos show before any screenshot. (Steamworks docs)
- Valve recommends **trailer #1 = mostly gameplay**, seen from the camera the player actually plays with, with the **HUD on**.
- Viewers drag the progress bar looking for gameplay, so **gameplay starts on frame 1**: no studio logo, no cinematic intro (Derek Lieu, Chris Zukowski / GameDiscoverCo).
- Length: **60–90s** for a gameplay trailer, 30–45s for a teaser. Rough structure: 0–5s hook · 5–15s genre + perspective + core mechanic · 15–50s variety, features, progression · last ~10s climax → title → **Wishlist** CTA.
- It has to work **without sound**: short captions over the footage, no full-screen text slides.
- Spec: 1920×1080, 30/60fps, H.264 + AAC, ≥5 Mbps.
- Video #2 is usually a short hype teaser or one unedited stretch of gameplay. The old `brag-output/brag.mp4` (21s, logo up front, "COMING SOON") is closer to a teaser. It could go in slot #2 after a refresh.

## Angle
"Real 2v2 football, but the tackle button is a dropkick." Then show the depth: Cores → anime Ultimates → Main Path, teammates, costumes → online → clutch finish.
**Tone:** chaotic-but-readable. Steam viewers are buyers, not scrollers: every caption stays on screen long enough to read, and the camera zooms in (≥1.7×) so the small sprites are still readable inside Steam's small player.
